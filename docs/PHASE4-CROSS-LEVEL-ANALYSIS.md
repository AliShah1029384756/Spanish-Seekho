# Spanish Seekho --- Phase 4 Cross-Level Curriculum Analysis

**Date:** 28 November 2026\
**Scope:** A1--C2 curriculum progression, grammar, vocabulary,
assessment, multilingual scaffolding, structure and technical
consistency\
**Repository:** https://github.com/AliShah1029384756/Spanish-Seekho\
**Master audit:** `docs/CURRICULUM-AUDIT-LOG.md`\
**Mode:** Analysis and planning only. No project files changed. No
patch, implementation, or deployment performed.

## Executive summary

Spanish Seekho has a strong, intentional Spain-oriented curriculum and a
promising advanced pathway. Its approved 168-module map is a planning
framework---not evidence that 168 lessons are implemented or that CEFR
proficiency has been externally validated.

The main cross-level risks are systemic rather than isolated: 1. Grammar
appears before its formation/usage is fully taught in some places,
especially subjunctive forms and demonstratives. 2. Assessment design
reportedly relies too much on English-language meta-questions and
answer-length cues, with the bias worsening at higher levels. 3.
Spanish-medium comprehension and independent production
(speaking/writing) are under-measured relative to multiple-choice
recognition. 4. Roman Urdu and pronunciation scaffolding appears
inconsistent, especially after A1/A2 and in B1/B2 example sentences. 5.
Spain-specific morphology (vosotros) and register distinctions are not
sustained across levels. 6. Learner-facing module counts, status notes,
and review/checkpoint labels need reconciliation. 7. A few source-level
contradictions can be verified; other language findings in the audit log
remain reviewer-reported and need a full-context pass before being
considered confirmed.

**Recommended approach:** targeted, dependency-aware corrections---not
wholesale rewriting. Repair prerequisites and labels first, then align
assessments, translations/pronunciation, and finally verify the full
learning path in browser/live conditions.

## Evidence and verification policy

-   **Source-verified:** inspected actual source excerpt confirms the

-   **Reported---verify:** documented in the master audit, but not
    independently checked in full source context during this Phase 4

-   **Needs deeper review:** plausible risk or opportunity, not yet a

-   **Not live-verified:** no browser walkthrough, deployed-site QA,

### 3. Progression principles for future repair

-   A concept may be previewed as a useful chunk before a full
    explanation, but the page must label it as a chunk and provide a
    later explicit teach-and-practise point.
-   Each later occurrence should add something identifiable: new
    function, contrast, register, discourse use, complexity, or
    production demand.
-   Review should spiral through retrieval and use---not merely repeat
    the same sentence or quiz format.
-   Higher levels must be harder because the language task is harder,
    not because the English meta-question is more abstract.
-   "Can-do" outcomes must be supported by evidence from learners'
    performance, not just lesson completion or quiz scores.

## C. Master gap list

The IDs below are new Phase 4 consolidation IDs. Earlier IDs are
retained in parentheses to cross-reference the master register.

### Category 1 --- Confirmed errors

**CONF-01 --- A1 L22 colour plural rule is overgeneralized**\
- Level: A1\
- Location: `a1/lesson-22.html`, colour/agreement explanation\
- Evidence: Source text states "In the plural, add -es" and illustrates
`camisas azules` and `pantalones verdes`. The examples are correct, but
the rule does not distinguish vowel-final adjectives (typically add
`-s`, e.g. `verde → verdes`) from consonant-final adjectives such as
`azul → azules`.\
- Why it matters: The learner may apply the wrong plural rule to
adjectives ending in vowels.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Recommended correction: State the two patterns with clear examples and
avoid presenting `-es` as universal.\
- Verification: **Source-verified** in selected HTML excerpt; full page
context still should be checked before repair.

**CONF-02 --- A2 index availability/count copy conflicts with README and
reported inventory**\
- Level: A2\
- Location: `a2/index.html` intro/availability status; `README.md` A2
pathway section; lesson inventory\
- Evidence: A2 index text states "20 starter lessons available" and the
eyebrow says "LESSONS 01--20 AVAILABLE"; README and audit inventory
report 23 core lessons.\
- Why it matters: Learners may believe lessons 21--23 are unavailable,
or progress/counting becomes inconsistent.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Recommended correction: Establish the actual current core lesson
count, distinguish core lessons from closing modules, then align index,
progress counter, README and completion messages.\
- Verification: **Source-verified** mismatch between retrieved index and
README; lesson inventory count should be independently enumerated before
changing.

**CONF-03 --- C2 status wording is internally contradictory**\
- Level: C2\
- Location: `c2/index.html`, pathway status paragraph\
- Evidence: Text says "C2 Units 01--03 Lessons 01--12 are available" and
also says "All 24 core lessons and four closing modules are available."\
- Why it matters: First clause implies only 12 lessons are available;
second claims all 24.\
- Severity: **Low**\
- Recommended correction: Replace residual partial-availability wording
with one accurate statement consistent with the actual links and
progress count.\
- Verification: **Source-verified** on fetched index excerpt.

### Category 2 --- Cross-level progression gaps

**PROG-01 --- Subjunctive prerequisites are not reliably sequenced**\
- Levels: B1--C2\
- Locations reported: B1 L10, L20, L21, L24; B2 L02, L07, L10, L11, L14,
L15, L23; C1/C2 advanced lessons\
- Evidence: Audit reports imperfect, perfect and pluperfect subjunctive
forms appearing before their formation/trigger explanations. Present
subjunctive may omit stem/spelling-change patterns despite using them in
examples.\
- Why: Learners cannot reliably interpret or independently form
mood/tense combinations.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Correction: Map subjunctive triggers → present forms →
irregular/stem/spelling changes → perfect → imperfect → pluperfect →
si-clauses/complex subordination; mark early examples as
"recognition/chunk" when intentional.\
- Verification: **Reported---verify across all referenced lessons.**

**PROG-02 --- B2 grammar appears before the lesson that explains it**\
- Level: B2\
- Locations: B2 L01--08 versus reported teaching in L09--12; examples
include `vaya a funcionar` (L06) and `sería recomendable que… ampliara`
(L07).\
- Why: Learners meet complex forms before the intended teaching,
increasing cognitive load.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Correction: Either move the prerequisite lessons earlier, add a
concise bridge, or mark the form as previewed and revisit it
explicitly.\
- Verification: **Reported---verify sequencing in full.**

**PROG-03 --- Reported speech and backshift prerequisite may be late**\
- Levels: B1--B2\
- Location: B1 L23; B2 later development\
- Evidence: Reported audit says backshift is used at B1 without prior
explicit formation teaching.\
- Why: Learners may memorize isolated forms without understanding
time/reference shifts.\
- Severity: **Medium**\
- Correction: Introduce basic reported statements with supported tense
changes and pronoun/time adjustments; reserve more complex reporting for
B2.\
- Verification: **Reported---verify.**

**PROG-04 --- Vosotros is not sustained after the regional-variation
lesson**\
- Levels: A2--C2\
- Locations: A1/A2 exposure; B1/B2 occurrences reported (B1 L19, B2
L06/L13); C1 L16; C2 generally rare\
- Why: A Spain-oriented course should make vosotros forms accessible in
ordinary contexts, not treat them as a one-off regional feature.\
- Severity: **Medium**\
- Correction: Spiral vosotros pronouns, present conjugation, imperative,
subjunctive and `os` in natural dialogues, clearly contrasting familiar
ustedes forms where helpful.\
- Verification: **Reported---verify frequency and first teaching
point.**

**PROG-05 --- Pronoun/clitic system needs a coherent cross-level map**\
- Levels: A1--B2 (then advanced usage)\
- Locations: A1/A2 examples and reported `Te lo envío`; later B1/B2
narrative, requests and subordinate clauses\
- Why: Object pronouns and combinations are high-frequency and
difficult; isolated chunks without a progression can lead to fossilized
errors.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Correction: Build a sequence from reflexive/direct/indirect object
pronouns → placement with finite verbs/infinitives/gerunds/imperatives →
combinations and `se lo` → pronoun use inside complex clauses.\
- Verification: **Needs deeper review; not confirmed as a
curriculum-wide gap.**

**PROG-06 --- Checkpoint sequence is confusing at A2**\
- Level: A2\
- Locations: A2 L08, L19, L20, L21--23 and closing modules\
- Evidence: Audit reports review/checkpoint/final-practice labels
appearing before further new lessons.\
- Why: "Review", "checkpoint", or "final" signals a stage that learners
may misunderstand.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Correction: Reorder or relabel mid-course checkpoints as progress
checks; reserve final assessment/readiness language for the end.\
- Verification: **Reported---verify exact current navigation.**

**PROG-07 --- B1 si-clauses may be over-demanding for productive B1**\
- Level: B1\
- Location: B1 L10 reportedly uses `si tuviera/viviera`\
- Why: Recognition preview may be suitable, but full independent
production may exceed the intended stage without a clear bridge.\
- Severity: **Medium**\
- Correction: Define whether learners are expected to recognize,
complete, or independently produce the structure; consider productive
mastery at B2 while previewing at B1.\
- Verification: **Reported---curriculum-placement judgment, not a
confirmed error.**

**PROG-08 --- Level transitions need performance gates, not completion
counts**\
- Levels: A1--C2\
- Location: Course checkpoints, final self-assessments/readiness
modules\
- Evidence: Course has lesson progress and self-assessment; README
states speaking/writing are self-reviewed and not automatically graded
in A2.\
- Why: Completion and multiple-choice scores alone cannot demonstrate
CEFR speaking, writing, interaction, mediation or listening.\
- Severity: **High**\
- Correction: Define short observable "can do" tasks, rubrics and
examples for each level exit; keep claims honest (course checkpoint, not
certification).\
- Verification: **Structure verified from README/master plan; exact
performance criteria need review.**

offline, mobile/RTL and accessibility behavior: not tested in this pass.

## H. Final recommendation

The next phase should **not** begin with lesson rewriting. Start with a
baseline inventory and a compact curriculum mapping spreadsheet or
register. That enables the team to see where each grammar concept is
introduced, taught, practised, assessed and recycled across all six
levels.

Then repair in this order:

1.  Reconcile inventories and learner-facing status copy.
2.  Correct confirmed language/translation errors.
3.  Repair grammar prerequisites and first-use sequencing.
4.  Redesign quiz quality and productive assessment.
5.  Standardize multilingual/pronunciation support.
6.  Strengthen Spain vocabulary, vosotros and register progression.
7.  Clarify review/closing module structure and reduce repetition.
8.  Run source, browser, live, mobile, RTL, TTS, offline and regression
    verification.

**Bottom line:** preserve the course's identity and advanced strengths.
The most valuable improvement is to make the entire A1--C2 pathway feel
like one deliberately scaffolded journey---with accurate teaching before
use, meaningful spiraling, credible assessments and consistent learner
support---rather than six collections of lessons.
