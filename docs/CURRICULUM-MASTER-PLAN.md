# Spanish Seekho — CEFR Curriculum Master Plan

_Status: Approved target; planned, not fully implemented._  
_Last updated: 24 November 2026_

## 1. Mission

**Making Spanish Learning Accessible to Every Pakistani.**

Spanish Seekho is a free, practical, mobile-first Spanish-learning platform for Pakistani learners, including people preparing to live, study or work in Spain. Teach contemporary standard Spanish from Spain (Castilian). Use simple Roman Urdu as the default explanation language, with Urdu-script and English support where the product supports them. Roman Urdu/Urdu pronunciation spellings are approximate learning aids, not phonetic transcription.

## 2. Approved programme architecture

**168 modules total: 28 modules at each of six levels (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2).**

This 168-module count is Spanish Seekho's own curriculum design decision. CEFR defines proficiency levels and descriptors; it does not prescribe a lesson count.

Each level contains:
- 6 progressive units × 4 core teaching modules = 24 modules.
- Module 25: cumulative revision and targeted gap repair.
- Module 26: action-oriented practical project.
- Module 27: integrated skills assessment.
- Module 28: end-of-level checkpoint, self-assessment and next-level readiness plan.

A module is a coherent learning experience, not a page padded to reach a target count. Use varied formats when pedagogically appropriate.

## 3. Reference framework and honest claims

Use the Council of Europe's CEFR Companion Volume (2020) and searchable descriptors as the proficiency framework. Use the Instituto Cervantes Plan curricular (PCIC) as the Spanish-specific reference for objectives, grammar, pragmatic functions, notions, cultural content and vocabulary.

Official references:
- CEFR Companion Volume: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions
- CEFR descriptors: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors
- CEFR Companion Volume PDF: https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4
- Instituto Cervantes Plan curricular index: https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/plan_curricular/indice.htm
- Instituto Cervantes introduction: https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/plan_curricular/introduccion.htm

The curriculum must cover reception (listening/reading), production (speaking/writing), interaction (spoken/written) and mediation, alongside linguistic, sociolinguistic, pragmatic and intercultural competence.

**Claims policy**
- The 168-module map is a plan, not evidence that the modules exist.
- Describe a pathway as CEFR-aligned only after a documented descriptor-to-content audit and quality review.
- Never claim Council of Europe or Instituto Cervantes accreditation, endorsement or certification.
- A website quiz score is not proof of CEFR proficiency. Call it a course checkpoint or self-assessment.
- Do not market an in-site completion certificate as an externally recognised language qualification.

## 4. Level exit outcomes

- **A1 — Beginner:** Understand and use familiar everyday expressions; introduce self and others; ask and answer simple personal questions; interact simply when the other person speaks slowly and helpfully.
- **A2 — Elementary:** Understand frequently used expressions about immediate needs; handle routine exchanges; describe background, surroundings and immediate needs in simple connected language.
- **B1 — Intermediate:** Understand main points of clear standard input on familiar matters; manage many travel situations; produce connected text; describe experiences and give brief reasons/explanations.
- **B2 — Upper-intermediate:** Understand complex texts on concrete and abstract topics; interact with fluency and spontaneity; explain and defend viewpoints in detailed text.
- **C1 — Advanced:** Understand demanding longer texts and implicit meaning; use Spanish flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes; produce well-structured detailed communication.
- **C2 — Highly proficient:** Understand virtually everything heard or read; synthesise sources coherently; express fine shades of meaning precisely, fluently and appropriately.

These are practical summaries. Lesson design must consult the actual level-specific CEFR descriptors, not rely on these summaries alone.

## 6. Standard teaching-module blueprint

Every teaching module should include, where appropriate:
- Stable ID, CEFR level, module number, title, topic and prerequisites.
- Two to five measurable “By the end, you can…” outcomes.
- A meaningful real-life context or action-oriented task.
- Accurate Spanish, natural translations and a focused language/communication point.
- Simple Roman Urdu explanation before independent learner use.
- Contextual examples, not isolated rules alone.
- Practice with clear instructions and feedback/answer key where objectively markable.
- Recycling of prior vocabulary/grammar.
- A short learner self-check.

Add vocabulary, dialogue, reading/listening text, Spain usage note, pronunciation aid, speaking prompt, writing task, mediation activity, quiz or project rubric when useful. Do not claim audio or automated speaking assessment unless it exists and has been tested. Do not force identical sections into every module.

## 7. Assessment standard

Module quizzes may use 8–12 questions where appropriate, but a multiple-choice score alone cannot demonstrate language proficiency.

Module 27 at each level should sample:
- Listening and reading comprehension.
- Spoken production and interaction.
- Written production.
- Mediation: explaining, relaying or summarising information.
- Appropriate linguistic range, accuracy, fluency/coherence and pragmatic choices.

Module 28 should give a transparent readiness profile with strengths and areas to practise. A simple website quiz threshold (for example 70–75%) may be a product completion indicator, never a CEFR proficiency threshold. Keep later lessons accessible; do not gate course access behind quiz completion.

## 8. Spain Spanish and Pakistani learner policy

- Teach contemporary standard Spanish from Spain, including vosotros forms, with accurate explanations of ustedes and regional/social variation.
- Explain that c/z pronunciation differs across Spanish varieties; do not imply every speaker in Spain or the Spanish-speaking world uses one identical accent.
- Teach tú/usted, politeness, turn-taking and register in context.
- Use Spain-specific contexts (transport, housing, healthcare, education, appointments and public services) while clearly labelling regional vocabulary.
- Use learner-friendly comparisons where accurate; do not assume all Pakistani learners share the same first language.
- Translate meaning naturally, not mechanically word-for-word.
- Label Roman Urdu/Urdu pronunciation spellings as approximations.

## 9. Spain Life companion pathway

Spain Life is a practical companion pathway, not a seventh CEFR level and not a substitute for general language proficiency. Existing documented modules: transport, healthcare, airport, housing, public offices and shopping.

Potential future additions include education/enrolment, job search, banking, mobile services, utilities, rental communication, emergencies, social etiquette and understanding official letters/forms. Tag activities with suggested CEFR prerequisites and link them to relevant course lessons. Language content must not present legal, immigration, medical or financial explanations as authoritative professional advice.

## 10. Quality and release gates

A level is not complete merely because 28 pages exist.

**Curriculum**
- Every module maps to explicit level outcomes.
- The level has coherent progression and no major gaps/duplication.
- Reception, production, interaction and mediation are represented.
- PCIC inventories and CEFR descriptors have been consulted.
- Spain Life complements rather than replaces the core course.

**Language**
- Check spelling, accents, punctuation, gender, agreement, verb forms and pronoun use.
- Check natural Castilian usage, register and pragmatic context.
- Check Roman Urdu explanations and translations.
- Label pronunciation approximations.
- Obtain proficient/experienced Spanish review where feasible, especially for advanced content.

**Assessment**
- Answer keys are correct and defensible alternatives accepted.
- Productive tasks have clear criteria.
- End-of-level checks sample more than grammar recognition.
- Results are described honestly and never as official certification.

**Product and engineering**
- Preserve free static HTML/CSS/vanilla-JS architecture unless a documented decision approves change.
- Preserve direct lesson URLs and all existing progress keys.
- Do not gate access to later lessons.
- Check Android/mobile usability, accessibility, keyboard focus, Urdu RTL and Spanish LTR.
- Check GitHub Pages project-relative paths.
- Review service-worker cache list/version if app-shell assets change.
- Test offline behaviour on a real device/browser before claiming it works.
- Record only tests actually performed.

## 13. Production and change-control rules

1. Read AGENTS.md, PROJECT-HANDOFF.md, ROADMAP.md, this master plan, the playbook and batch plan.
2. Inspect actual latest code and status before choosing modules.
3. Produce coherent batches, normally five complete lessons.
4. Review sequence, level, duplication, language and answer keys as a batch.
5. Integrate only after checking existing file conventions and progress behaviour.
6. Run available checks and record exactly what was tested.
7. Update the status matrix, handoff, roadmap and next batch.
8. Multiple AIs may write non-overlapping ranges only; one integrator owns final review and integration.

The 168-module target and six-level structure are approved decisions. Changes to the total, level structure, language policy or assessment model require an explicit owner decision and an update to this document. Individual titles/order may be refined when an audit or expert review demonstrates a better progression; record the reason and preserve outcomes.
