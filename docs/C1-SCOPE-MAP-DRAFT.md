## 1. C1 exit profile

A C1 learner should be able to use Spanish flexibly and effectively for social, academic and professional purposes; understand demanding, longer texts and implicit meaning; and produce clear, detailed, well-structured communication. This is a practical programme target informed by CEFR descriptors, not a claim of formal CEFR validation.

C1 must be more than “B2 grammar with harder vocabulary”. The progression should emphasise:
- interpreting implication, stance, presupposition, bias, rhetorical purpose and subtle shifts in tone;
- synthesising long, dense and sometimes specialised sources while weighing perspectives;
- adapting register, style, structure and level of detail to audience, purpose and medium;
- leading complex discussions, negotiating disagreement and facilitating shared decisions;
- mediating specialised concepts and complex arguments for audiences with different knowledge;
- controlling advanced grammar, lexical nuance, discourse cohesion and phonological clarity in extended communication.

## 3. Provisional CEFR coverage map

The CEFR Companion Volume organises communicative activity around **reception, production, interaction and mediation**. Traditional listening/reading and speaking/writing remain important, but online interaction, mediation and phonological competence also deserve explicit attention. Map these strands across the level rather than isolating them in the final assessment.

| C1 strand | Primary modules | Evidence to design into activities |
|---|---|---|
| Reception — reading and listening | 09–12, 24, 27 | Long/demanding input, implied meaning, stance, bias, argument structure and source synthesis. |
| Production — spoken and written | 04–07, 14, 20, 24, 26–27 | Extended explanations, reports, proposals, presentations and controlled rhetorical structure. |
| Interaction — spoken, written and online | 05–08, 13, 15–16, 21, 23–24 | Turn management, collaborative decisions, tactful challenge, asynchronous discussion and audience adaptation. |
| Mediation — text, concepts and communication | 08, 12, 21–24, 26–27 | Accurate summaries, explanation of specialised ideas, reconciling viewpoints and facilitating understanding. |
| Linguistic and pragmatic competence | 01–04, 14, 17–20 | Nuance, discourse cohesion, lexical precision, syntax, qualification and rhetorical effect. |
| Sociolinguistic and intercultural competence | 13, 15–16, 21, 23 | Register, indirectness, humour, variation and intercultural repair. |
| Phonological competence | 04–06, 11, 21, 24, 26–27 | Intelligibility, stress, rhythm, pausing, intonation and listener-oriented delivery. |

This is a proposed design map, not a formal descriptor-by-descriptor validation. During lesson authoring, select specific C1 descriptors for each outcome and retain an evidence note explaining why the task demonstrates the descriptor.

## 4. B2 → C1 progression rules

| B2 foundation | C1 step-up |
|---|---|
| Defend a clear viewpoint with relevant reasons | Weigh competing interpretations, implicit assumptions and limits of evidence before taking a nuanced position |
| Compare a manageable set of sources | Synthesise longer, denser and sometimes specialised sources while preserving attribution and disagreement |
| Participate fluently in professional discussion | Chair, facilitate and redirect complex multi-party discussion towards a shared outcome |
| Adapt formal/informal tone | Control register, indirectness, irony, connotation and audience expectations with greater flexibility |
| Use advanced grammar accurately in context | Choose grammar and information structure for subtle stance, rhetorical distance and stylistic effect |
| Summarise and mediate structured information | Explain specialised concepts and mediate between people with different knowledge, priorities or cultural assumptions |
| Complete a B2 online discussion task in Module 27 | Practise online interaction repeatedly across C1 units, including asynchronous collaboration and response to feedback |
| Use a basic speaking self-review rubric | Build a progressive phonological profile using intelligibility, stress, rhythm, phrasing and intonation evidence with human feedback |

## 5. Authoring pattern and quality controls

Each C1 module should be a coherent learning experience, not a page padded to satisfy a fixed word or example count. Where appropriate, preserve the proven Spanish Seekho lesson conventions: clear outcomes, explicit teaching, contextual Spanish examples with translations, listening support, meaningful production, a reasoned knowledge check, progress persistence and open navigation.

C1-specific requirements:
1. Use original or appropriately licensed texts. Do not reproduce long copyrighted articles, lectures or books; create authentic-style source packs or short attributed extracts where permitted.
2. Make implicit meaning and critical interpretation teachable: show the textual clue, not just the answer.
3. Include listening and reading of varied genres: interviews, lectures, reports, editorials, professional briefings and public discussion.
4. Integrate online written interaction and mediation across multiple units, not only in Module 27.
5. Teach Spain Spanish as the main variety while explicitly contextualising relevant wider Spanish variation in Module 16.
6. Use a human-friendly speaking/writing rubric. Text-to-speech may support listening and modelling; it is not a pronunciation assessment.
7. Review every answer key, distractor, explanation, translation and register choice for ambiguity and accuracy.
8. Keep progress local and transparent, preserve stable IDs, and avoid locking access behind arbitrary scores.
9. Describe the course as CEFR-informed learning material, never as an official CEFR exam, accredited qualification or externally validated certificate.
