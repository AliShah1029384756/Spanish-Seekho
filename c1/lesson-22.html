CEFR C1 · UNIT 06 · LESSON 22

Explaining specialised concepts to different audiences

Translate technical or abstract knowledge into accessible Spanish without losing accuracy, nuance or essential caveats.

C1 advancedRoman Urdu supportSpain Spanish
LEARNING OUTCOMES

By the end, you can…

  • Assess an audience's prior knowledge and communicative needs.
  • Explain specialist terms through definitions, examples, analogies and reformulation.
  • Adapt detail and register while preserving accuracy and limitations.
PART 1 · HIGH-LEVEL INTERACTION

Diagnose the audience

Before explaining, consider what listeners already know, what they need to do and how much detail is useful. Para un público no especializado… frames the level.

Para un público no especializado, conviene empezar por la idea general.For a non-specialist audience, it is best to start with the general idea.
El equipo técnico necesita conocer también los límites del sistema.The technical team also needs to know the system's limitations.
PART 2 · HIGH-LEVEL INTERACTION

Define a term precisely

Introduce a term, give a concise definition and connect it to a familiar context. Por… entendemos… makes boundaries clear.

Por aprendizaje automático entendemos un sistema que identifica patrones a partir de datos.By machine learning we mean a system that identifies patterns from data.
En este contexto, la latencia es el tiempo que tarda una respuesta en llegar.In this context, latency is the time a response takes to arrive.
PART 3 · HIGH-LEVEL INTERACTION

Use examples and analogies carefully

An analogy supports understanding but is not identical to the concept. Es parecido a…, aunque… signals both usefulness and limits.

Es parecido a un filtro, aunque el sistema no comprende la información como una persona.It is similar to a filter, although the system does not understand information like a person.
Un ejemplo sencillo sería comparar la memoria caché con una nota de consulta rápida.A simple example would be to compare cache memory with a quick-reference note.
PART 4 · HIGH-LEVEL INTERACTION

Reformulate and check comprehension

Restate an explanation in simpler terms, then invite a question. Dicho de forma más sencilla… reduces cognitive load.

Dicho de forma más sencilla, el cifrado convierte los datos en un formato que no se puede leer sin una clave.Put more simply, encryption turns data into a format that cannot be read without a key.
¿Quieres que lo explique con un ejemplo práctico?Would you like me to explain it with a practical example?
PART 5 · HIGH-LEVEL INTERACTION

Preserve caveats and uncertainty

Accessible language must not turn a probability into a guarantee. Esto no significa que… and los resultados dependen de… keep limitations visible.

Esto no significa que el modelo siempre vaya a acertar.This does not mean that the model will always be correct.
Los resultados dependen de la calidad y representatividad de los datos.The results depend on the quality and representativeness of the data.
PART 6 · HIGH-LEVEL INTERACTION

Adapt depth and register

For executives, highlight implications and decisions; for specialists, methods and assumptions. La cuestión clave para… es… focuses relevance.

La cuestión clave para la dirección es el impacto en los plazos y los costes.The key issue for management is the impact on deadlines and costs.
Desde el punto de vista metodológico, habría que ampliar la muestra.From a methodological point of view, the sample would need to be expanded.
PART 7 · INDEPENDENT PRODUCTION

Put the language to work

Choose one specialist topic you know. Write two explanations in Spanish: a 90-word version for a general audience and a 140-word version for specialists. Keep the core facts and limitations consistent, and explain what you changed.
